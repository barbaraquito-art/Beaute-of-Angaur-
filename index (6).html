Discover Angaur

Palau's Southernmost Hidden Paradise

Nestled at the southern tip of Palau's archipelago, Angaur is a world apart — a quiet island of just eight square kilometres where limestone cliffs meet turquoise waters, and ancient traditions thrive undisturbed.

With a population of barely 114 souls, this is one of the last truly untouched destinations in the Pacific. Here, the air carries the salt of the ocean, the forests hide centuries of history, and every corner of the island tells a story.

Four settlements divide the island, each with its own high chief and its own place in the traditional order.

Oral Tradition

The Legend of Uab

The Giant That Fell

Long ago, a boy was born on Angaur — ordinary at first, but consumed by an insatiable hunger. As he grew, so did his greed. He devoured everything the island could offer, outgrowing every house, every boundary.

The people, fearing for their children and their gardens, made a desperate choice. They bound him in his sleep and set the fires.

When Uab fell, his massive body shattered across the Pacific — and from his remains, the islands of Palau were born.

The legend endures as a reminder: unchecked desire destroys not just the one who carries it, but the entire community.

Depicted on the Belau National Museum Bai

Symbol of Unity

The Angaur State Flag

Designed by Leon Gulibert in 2000, the flag places the orange Kelau flower at its centre — a symbol of the customs and ceremonies practised only in Angaur.

Eighteen stamens radiate from within, each representing one of Angaur's eighteen clans. The four stripes honour the island's four hamlets and high chiefs, while the blue and green fields speak to the surrounding ocean and fertile land.

Plan Your Visit

Before You Arrive

Getting Here

  • Daily flights from Airai Airport via single-engine aircraft
  • Regina IV charter boat service available
  • 6,600-ft airstrip, originally Japanese-built

Permits & Fees

  • Permit fee required for site visits
  • Separate permit for photography and video filming
  • Contact Angaur State Government for arrangements

Visitor Guidelines

  • Take everything you bring when you leave
  • Do not remove anything from historical sites
  • Respect culturally sensitive areas at all times
  • Familiarise yourself with evacuation routes

Safety & Emergency

  • Designated evacuation centres for tsunamis, typhoons, and flooding
  • Know your nearest evacuation route before exploring
  • Follow official instructions during severe weather warnings
